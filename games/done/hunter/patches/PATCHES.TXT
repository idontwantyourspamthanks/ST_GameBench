What changed against the original release:
- Faster 3D view on a stock 8 MHz ST/STE, pixel-identical to the original:
  * the polygon filler was rewritten (rows outside the screen are skipped, edges are walked in blocks),
  * the edge set-up avoids the slow divide when it is not needed,
  * the joystick poll in the 50 Hz interrupt no longer waits 350 microseconds each time.
  Together this is about 6.5% (standing) to 13.7% (walking) fewer CPU cycles per frame.
- STE sound (STE, Mega STE, TT, Falcon; reworked in v1.3): the YM sound effects are replaced
  by the Amiga version's samples, played through the STE DMA sound chip (mono, 12517 Hz native
  on STE, Mega STE, TT and Falcon, two voices, with a 1-pole IIR low-pass smoothing filter
  matching the Amiga A500's analog filter). Engine pitch follows the speed using the Amiga's
  actual vehicle base periods, and all 21 effect durations match the Amiga (fixing the long
  looping seagull and early one-shot cutoffs). Costs about 0.2% of the CPU when nothing plays,
  about 10% for one engine voice, up to about 21% with two table-scaled voices. A plain ST
  keeps the original YM sound.
- 50/60 Hz: the original game counts display refreshes. It asks the shifter for 50 Hz when it starts, but wherever the
  screen runs at 60 Hz anyway (hardware or an adapter that ignores that, an emulator set to 60 Hz) everything that moves or
  counts down ran 20% too fast: walking and driving, turning, NPCs, projectiles, the game clock, stamina and message
  timers, and the length of the sound effects. Now the world runs in real time at 50 and 60 Hz (50 "world ticks" per second
  whatever the refresh; 5 of every 6 refreshes at 60 Hz). At 50 Hz the game plays exactly as before.
- Atari Jaguar pad (new in v1.1; STE and Falcon enhanced joystick ports, either port, two pads work too): the d-pad steers
  like the joystick and A or B is fire. Keys: C = Return, Option = Space, Pause = P, * = Esc, 1 = R, 2 = Help, 3 = L,
  4 = A, 5 = F, 6 = C, 7/9 = cursor up/down. Menus stay mouse-driven. Each button is read every 80 ms, so a very
  short tap can be missed. Machines without the ports (ST, Mega ST, Mega STE, TT) run exactly as before, with no cost.
- One-key items and weapon keys (new in v1.2, patch key_item): in the 3D view, 1-9 (top row or keypad) select the nine weapons
  in the order of the weapon row of the item table (1 = the first, 9 = the last); H = use a medical kit, G = use a fuel can,
  J = use the parachute. Each key does exactly what clicking that icon in the item table does (same counts, same effect);
  an item you do not own does nothing. The keys are ignored in the item table, the log book, the game options and while
  paused, so typing a name or a log entry is not affected. With a Jaguar pad (STE and Falcon): # = H, 0 = G, 8 = J.
- Controls guide on pause (new in v1.2, patch help_pause): press P (or the Jaguar pad's Pause) in the 3D view to pause and see a small
  panel listing the controls: the stick and fire, the game's own keys (Return, Space, Esc, R, ?, L, A, F, C, P, cursor up/down), the new
  one-key items and weapons (1-9, H, G, J) and, on machines with the pad ports, the Jaguar pad buttons. Press P again: the panel is
  removed and the screen is restored exactly; the game world is not touched (pausing already stops it) and the mouse pointer is hidden
  while the panel is up. It is only drawn into the 3D view's picture, so it works at 50 and 60 Hz, with the sky, and on the TT and Falcon.
- Amiga dashboard (new in v1.2, patch sky2_pnl; ST, Mega ST, STE and Mega STE with 2 MB or more): the dashboard uses the Amiga version's
  colours and art. With less than 2 MB, and on the TT and Falcon, the dashboard is unchanged.
- STE palette (new in v1.2, patches pal_fade, pal_load; STE, Mega STE, TT, Falcon): the game's colours are the Amiga's 12-bit
  values; the STE can show all 4096 where the ST only shows 512, so by day the picture is half a colour step brighter and the
  night and dusk fades are smoother. A plain ST keeps the original colours.
- The Amiga's sky and sea (new in v1.2, patches sky_*; ST, Mega ST, STE, Mega STE): above the horizon the sky has the Amiga's six
  colour bands, drawn exactly (every band changes colour on its own screen line), and the sea below it is a solid colour instead of
  a dither. The horizon follows the camera height. At dawn and dusk the sky bands take warmer colours (this tint is new, not from the
  Amiga). The dashboard is lit separately, so it keeps its day colours at night. Costs about 2% of the CPU. The TT and Falcon keep the
  original dithered sky (their video timing is not safe for the bands; the STE palette still applies there).
- Triple buffering (new in v1.2, patches tb_*; 68000 machines with 1 MB or more): the game draws into a third screen page, so it no
  longer waits for the screen between frames: the 3D view is up to about 9% faster. On the TT and Falcon the original two pages are kept.
- Even mouse pointer (new, patches ptr_*; every machine): the pointer moved by however many mouse reports had arrived since the last
  screen refresh. An emulator that sends them 53 times per second against the 50 Hz refresh made the pointer stop for one refresh and
  then jump double about every 0.3 s (at 60 Hz it stopped on about every third refresh), and the game halved only leftward and
  upward movement, so left and up were slower than right and down. Now the pointer moves once per refresh by the mean of the last three
  refreshes' motion, without losing or inventing any, and all four directions have the same speed (right and down are unchanged;
  left and up are as fast as they are). The pointer follows the mouse one refresh later and stops two refreshes after it.
- TT (new in v1.1): a TT booting from hard disk was taken for a Falcon by the sound code, which then used the Falcon's
  rate. It now plays at the STE rate, as on the floppy.
- Save and Load work on the hard disk (new in v1.2). PP's build had them switched off: its file hook ignored the
  write and always read, so "Save Game" wrote nothing and then read the file back over the game's memory, and the slot
  list showed PP's placeholder names ("Do not use / this / for / game / state / saving").
  Now the six slots and the save directory are ordinary files in the game's FILES folder: SAVES.DIR (224 bytes) and
  SLOT1.SAV ... SLOT6.SAV (51,328 bytes each). They are created on the first save when missing; names are typed in the
  game as before. A save that fails (disk full, write protected, no FILES folder) says so and leaves the old save and
  the slot list untouched; a slot file that is damaged or missing is refused with a message instead of being loaded.
  Loading restores the game exactly to the saved state. Each save is written through to the disk before the game
  continues, so switching off straight after a save keeps it. (Init Disk was not tested.)
  Only the hard-disk build: the floppy release keeps its own loader and saving, which is unchanged.
- Version line: the title screen shows a small, dim "v@VERSION@ enhanced by dad_prg" at the bottom, so this release can not be
  mistaken for the original. Nothing else on the screen changes.
- Hardening (v1.2.1): the YM I/O port direction is now set explicitly (no known failure). The STE sound patch writes PSG register 7
  as an absolute value ($FF, both I/O ports outputs) when it takes over the YM, instead of reading it back and OR-ing; the
  floppy drive select lines (port A) can not be left as inputs.
- Every patch checks the original bytes it replaces first. If anything is not as
  expected, nothing is changed and the game starts as before.
- Nothing else in the game is changed: keyboard, mouse buttons and joystick controls and saves work as in PP's release, and at 50 Hz so does the game speed.

How it is delivered:
- START.TOS is replaced by a patched copy (unpacked); it also reads the extra file FILES\P
  (patch code plus the sample bank) through the game's own disk loader, then starts the game.
  If FILES\P is missing the game starts unpatched.

Requirements and usage are as in README.TXT (1 MB RAM minimum).
Run START.TOS from a hard disk as before (PP's HDDRIVER / AHDI setup).  Saves go to FILES\SAVES.DIR and FILES\SLOT1..6.SAV
in the game's folder; give the disk a few seconds after saving before switching off (HDDRIVER writes lazily).
Tested on Hatari (cold boots to the 3D view, sky/palette/dashboard/buffering as in the table, keys): HD build on STE (TOS 1.62) at 1 MB and
2 MB, Mega STE (TOS 2.06) at 4 MB, ST (TOS 1.04) at 1 MB, TT (TOS 3.06) at 4 MB; save/load on the STE, Mega STE and TT; the Jaguar pad
on the STE and the Falcon (TOS 4.04, floppy); 50 and 60 Hz.
The even mouse pointer: motion measured refresh by refresh on the ST, STE, Mega STE and TT (HD build; STE also at 60 Hz); cold boots to the 3D view
with the item table opened and clicked with the pointer on all of those, and on the STE, TT and Falcon floppy builds.
Not tested on real hardware.
